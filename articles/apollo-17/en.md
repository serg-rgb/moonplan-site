Apollo 17 was the sixth and last Apollo expedition in which people walked on the Moon. It was the first to carry a scientist to the Moon, Harrison Schmitt. Together with the commander, Eugene Cernan, he spent three days in the Taurus-Littrow valley on the edge of Mare Serenitatis (Sea of Serenity). People have not landed on the Moon since December 1972: according to Science News, the next landing is planned for the Artemis IV mission in 2028.

## The crew

Commander Eugene Cernan, 38, was flying into space for the third time (before that, Gemini 9 and Apollo 10). Command module pilot Ronald Evans, 39, and lunar module pilot Harrison Schmitt, 37, a civilian scientist, were flying for the first time. The command module was called America and the lunar module Challenger. The backup crew were John Young, Stuart Roosa and Charles Duke.

## The first night launch

The launch was delayed by 2 hours 40 minutes because of a fault in the automatic launch sequencer. The Saturn V lifted off on December 7, 1972 at 05:33 UTC, at night Florida time. It was the first night launch of Apollo.

[[figure:launch]]

The same day, already on the way to the Moon, the crew photographed the Earth.

From launch to splashdown, 301 hours 51 minutes 59 seconds passed.

## Three days in the Taurus-Littrow valley

Challenger landed on December 11 at 19:54:57 UTC in the Taurus-Littrow valley, on the southeastern edge of Mare Serenitatis, at 20.2° N, 30.8° E. Cernan and Schmitt went out onto the surface three times, for 22 hours 4 minutes in total. In the lunar rover they drove 30 km, collected 110.52 kg of samples, set up the ALSEP science instruments and took many photographs.

[[figure:boulder]]

On December 14 at 22:54:37 UTC Challenger lifted off. The camera on the lunar rover, which was left in place, was aimed from Houston, and the liftoff from the Moon was seen live.

[[figure:liftoff]]

## What the Moon looked like from Earth that day

We calculated the phase for the moment of landing, December 11, 1972 at 19:54:57 UTC. From Earth the Moon was a waxing crescent, 30% lit: 5.98 days had passed since the new moon on December 5. The distance from the centre of the Earth to the centre of the Moon was 389,973 km.

## The return

On December 17 Ronald Evans made a spacewalk and, in three trips to the scientific instrument bay, retrieved the film cassettes of the camera and the lunar sounder. Apollo 17 splashed down on December 19 at 19:24:59 UTC, about 350 nautical miles southeast of the Samoan Islands and 6.5 km from the USS Ticonderoga. The America command module is on display at the Johnson Space Center in Houston.
