The far side of the Moon can never be seen from Earth: the Moon always keeps the same face turned toward us. Until 2019, no spacecraft had ever made a soft landing there. On January 3, 2019, China's Chang'e 4 did it.

## Why no one had landed there before

The main difficulty is communication. The Earth cannot be seen from the far side, so a signal cannot be sent directly. China solved this in advance: in May 2018 it launched the relay satellite Queqiao, through which a vehicle on the far side talks to Earth.

## Flight and landing

Chang'e 4 launched on December 8, 2018 and entered lunar orbit four days later. There it was checked out for 22 days while waiting for the Sun to rise over the landing site. On January 3, 2019, at 10:26 Beijing time (02:26 UTC), the spacecraft landed, according to a report by Chinese state media cited by The Planetary Society.

The chosen site was Von Kármán crater, inside the huge South Pole–Aitken basin. Its floor was once flooded by basaltic lavas. The spacecraft landed on a relatively small patch of such basalts, heavily mixed with ejecta from the neighbouring, relatively young Finsen crater, 73 km across.

[[figure:region]]

The team of the American LRO camera found the exact landing site by matching the images Chang'e 4 took during its descent with their own orbital images: 45.457° S, 177.589° E, accurate to 20 metres.

[[figure:site]]

## The Yutu-2 rover

The Yutu-2 rover drove off the lander. In late January 2019, LRO turned by 59 degrees to image the landing site at an angle. The rover took up just two pixels in the image, to the north of the lander, but the shadows of both vehicles were already visible beside them.

[[figure:oblique]]

On February 1, 2019, LRO passed almost directly over the landing site at an altitude of 82 km. The resolution was 0.85 m per pixel, and the rover turned out to be 29 metres northwest of the lander.

[[figure:nadir]]

## What is being sought on the far side

Scientists have long wanted to know whether the basalts of the far side differ from those brought back from the near side. According to the China National Space Administration, Chang'e 4 carries a visible and near-infrared spectrometer, and its measurements help answer this question. In the LRO team's assessment, combining measurements on the surface and from orbit gives more than either one alone.

Chang'e 4 itself is almost a copy of its predecessor, Chang'e 3, which landed on the near side in 2013.

## What the Moon looked like from Earth at the moment of landing

We calculated the phase for January 3, 2019, 02:26 UTC. From Earth the Moon was a thin waning crescent, 8% lit: about three days remained until the new moon. The far side, by contrast, was almost entirely lit by the Sun: when an almost new Moon is visible from Earth, it is daytime on the far side. The distance from the centre of the Earth to the centre of the Moon was 394,709 km.
