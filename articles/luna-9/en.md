On February 3, 1966, the Soviet Luna 9 station became the first in history to make a soft landing on the Moon and send pictures of its surface to Earth. According to ESA, it was the first soft landing on any other celestial body at all.

## How the station was built

According to NASA's description, the station that landed on the Moon weighed 99 kg. It was a sealed container holding radio equipment, a programming unit, a thermal control system, scientific instruments, power sources and a television system.

A rocket put it into Earth orbit, and a fourth stage, which then separated, sent it on towards the Moon. Shortly before landing, the flight equipment separated from the station.

[[figure:lander]]

## The landing

According to NSSDCA, the station was launched on January 31, 1966, and on February 3 at 18:44:52 UTC it landed in Oceanus Procellarum (Ocean of Storms), at 7.08° N, 64.37° W.

After landing, the four petals that made up the station's shell opened outwards and set it firmly on the surface. The spring-loaded antennas moved into their working position, and a television camera with a rotating and tilting mirror began to photograph the surroundings.

## The first pictures from the surface of the Moon

The station held seven radio communication sessions, totalling 8 hours 5 minutes, and transmitted three series of television pictures. They were assembled into a panorama of the surroundings, which shows rocks next to the station and a horizon about 1.4 km away from it.

## What the Moon looked like from Earth that day

We calculated the phase at the moment of landing, February 3, 1966 at 18:44:52 UTC. From Earth the Moon was almost full and 95% lit: less than two days remained until full moon. The distance from the centre of the Earth to the centre of the Moon was 361,043 km.
