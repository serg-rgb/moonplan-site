According to NASA, Apollo 8 was the first crewed spacecraft to leave the vicinity of Earth and reach the Moon, and the first of the Apollo series to enter lunar orbit. The crew did not plan to land: there was no lunar module on board, only a mock-up of the same mass, 9,027 kg.

## The crew

Commander Frank Borman, command module pilot James Lovell and lunar module pilot William Anders.

[[figure:crew]]

## The launch

Saturn V lifted off on December 21, 1968 at 12:51 UTC. It was the first crewed launch of this rocket. First the spacecraft entered an orbit around Earth at an altitude of about 190 km, and at 15:41:37 UTC the third stage sent it toward the Moon.

[[figure:launch]]

## Flight timeline

Times are in UTC, as in the NASA archive.

| Date and time, UTC | Event |
|---|---|
| December 21, 12:51 | Launch from Complex 39A |
| December 21, 15:41:37 | The third stage sends the spacecraft toward the Moon |
| December 24, 09:59:20 | Entry into an elliptical lunar orbit, 310.6 × 111.2 km |
| December 24 | Two orbits later, a nearly circular orbit of about 110 × 112 km |
| December 25, 06:10:16 | The engine fires for the return to Earth, 10 orbits completed in total |
| December 27, 15:51:42 | Splashdown in the Pacific Ocean |

From launch to splashdown, 147 hours 0 minutes 42 seconds passed.

## Earthrise

The crew photographed both the near side and the far side of the Moon, gathering information on terrain and landmarks for future landings. On December 24, around 15:40 UTC, a photo was taken showing Earth rising over the lunar horizon.

[[figure:earthrise]]

We calculated what the Moon looked like from Earth at that moment: a waxing crescent, 30% illuminated, 4.89 days after the new moon of December 19. The distance from the center of Earth to the center of the Moon was 377,864 km.

## Christmas in lunar orbit

During the flight the crew made six live TV broadcasts. The best known is the Christmas Eve one, in which the astronauts read a passage from the Book of Genesis from lunar orbit.

## The return

Apollo 8 splashed down on December 27 at 15:51:42 UTC in the Pacific Ocean, about 1,000 miles south-southwest of Hawaii and 5 km from the aircraft carrier USS Yorktown. The command module is on display at the Museum of Science and Industry in Chicago.
