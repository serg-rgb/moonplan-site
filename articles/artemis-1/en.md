Artemis I is the first flight of the programme meant to return people to the Moon. There was no crew on board. According to NASA's description, it was the first joint test of the SLS (Space Launch System) rocket, the Orion spacecraft and the ground systems.

## The launch

The SLS rocket launched on November 16, 2022 at 1:47 US East Coast time (06:47 UTC) from Launch Complex 39B at the Kennedy Space Center.

[[figure:launch]]

## At the Moon

On November 20 the spacecraft entered the Moon's sphere of influence: from then on the Moon, not the Earth, was the main gravitational force acting on it. On November 21 Orion passed about 80 miles (about 130 km) from the surface, the closest approach of the flight, and then moved onto a distant retrograde orbit around the Moon.

[[figure:farside]]

The cameras at the ends of the solar arrays photographed the spacecraft against the Earth and the Moon.

[[figure:earth-moon]]

On December 5 Orion passed over the Moon again, about 79 miles (about 127 km) from the surface, with an engine burn, and set course back to the Earth.

[[figure:approach]]

## The return

Orion splashed down on December 11, 2022 at 12:40 US East Coast time off San Diego. The whole flight lasted 25 days 10 hours 53 minutes. The landing and recovery team worked from the US Navy ship USS Portland.

[[figure:recovery]]

## What the Moon looked like from Earth on the launch day

We calculated the phase for the moment of launch, November 16, 2022 at 06:47 UTC. From the Earth the Moon was waning, 53% lit, close to last quarter. The distance from the centre of the Earth to the centre of the Moon was 402,384 km.
