Artemis II is the first flight of people to the Moon since Apollo. NASA calls it the first crewed flyby of the Moon in 50 years. The crew did not land on the Moon. Their job was to fly around it, test the Orion spacecraft with people on board, and make observations that a person does better than a camera.

## The crew

Commander Reid Wiseman, pilot Victor Glover and mission specialist Christina Koch from NASA, and mission specialist Jeremy Hansen from the Canadian Space Agency.

## The launch

The SLS rocket with Orion launched on April 1, 2026 at 6:35 p.m. US East Coast time from Launch Complex 39B at the Kennedy Space Center.

[[figure:launch]]

## Seven hours at the Moon

On April 6 the crew flew around the Moon. According to Science News, the astronauts spent about seven hours making scientific observations near the Moon: they described its colors and terrain and watched a solar eclipse, when the Moon covered the Sun.

[[figure:earthset]]

[[figure:earthset-2]]

The eclipse lasted 54 minutes. During that time the crew saw the solar corona, interplanetary dust, stars and planets. They also saw five flashes on the night side of the Moon from small meteoroids hitting it. According to the science team's estimate, quoted by Science News, these were bodies of about 8.5 g and a little over 4.5 cm across, roughly the size of a golf ball. The science team believes the crew set a record: these are the smallest meteorites whose impact on the Moon has been spotted by eye or by camera.

## The colors of the Moon and the terminator

According to Science News, one of the main goals was the Moon's subdued colors: the human eye tells them apart better than a camera. The crew described how the colors change with viewing angle and lighting. Scientists link these shades to the composition of the surface: brown tones reflect different iron content, green ones reflect glass formed in eruptions or impacts.

The astronauts were especially struck by the terminator, the boundary between day and night on the Moon. According to the science team, the lighting there is similar to what future expeditions will find near the Moon's south pole, where the Sun stands low and shadows are long.

## The return

Orion splashed down on April 10, 2026 in the Pacific Ocean off San Diego. According to NASA, the flight lasted 9 days 1 hour 32 minutes.

## What the Moon looked like from Earth during the flyby

We calculated the phase for April 6, 2026 at 22:41 UTC, the moment of one of the Earth-set photos. From Earth the Moon was waning, 79% illuminated. The distance from the center of Earth to the center of the Moon was 404,865 km.

## What's next

According to Science News, the next crewed landing on the Moon is planned for the Artemis IV mission in 2028, on landers from Blue Origin or SpaceX.
