Apollo 11 flew for a little over eight days. In that time two people walked on the Moon for the first time, spent almost a day on its surface and came home with 21.55 kg of lunar rocks and soil. Below is how it went hour by hour, from the NASA archive.

## Crew

Commander Neil Armstrong, lunar module pilot Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin and command module pilot Michael Collins. For all three it was a second spaceflight: before that they had flown Gemini spacecraft (Armstrong on Gemini 8, Collins on Gemini 10, Aldrin on Gemini 12). The backup crew was Jim Lovell, Fred Haise and William Anders.

The spacecraft had two parts with their own call signs. The command and service modules were called Columbia, the lunar module Eagle.

From launch to splashdown, 195 hours 18 minutes 35 seconds passed.

## Landing

The lunar module landed in the Sea of Tranquility, at 0.67° north latitude and 23.47° east longitude. Armstrong reported: "Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed." Six and a half hours later he climbed down the ladder and said the words that everyone has quoted since: "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind." Aldrin followed 19 minutes later.

[[figure:as11-footpad]]

From Armstrong's first step to closing the hatch, 2 hours 15 minutes passed. The astronauts set up scientific instruments (among them a seismometer and a retroreflector for laser ranging), took photographs and collected samples. In total they walked about 250 metres.

## What the Moon looked like from Earth that day

We calculated the phase at the moment of landing, July 20, 1969 at 20:17:40 UTC. From Earth the Moon was a waxing crescent, 33% lit. 6.25 days had passed since the new moon on July 14, and the first quarter came on July 22, when the crew was already flying home. The distance from the centre of the Earth to the centre of the Moon at that moment was 389,456 km.

This is not an accident. The landing was planned for lunar morning, when the Sun stands low over the landing site and rocks and craters cast long shadows, which makes it easier for the pilot to judge the terrain. And lunar morning over the Sea of Tranquility falls on the days when the waxing Moon is visible from Earth shortly before first quarter.

## What came back

21.55 kg of lunar rocks and soil. These were the first samples from another celestial body that people brought to Earth themselves. The Columbia command module is now on display at the National Air and Space Museum in Washington.

## What came next

Apollo 11 was the first of six landings. After it, five more crews visited the Moon, the last one on Apollo 17 in December 1972. In all, 12 people have walked on the Moon. We cover each mission separately.
