Four months after Apollo 11, a second crew flew to the Moon. The task was harder: to land not merely at a safe site but at a chosen point, next to the uncrewed Surveyor 3, which had landed on the Moon two and a half years earlier. The landing succeeded, and the flight is remembered for the fact that lightning struck the rocket twice in its first minute.

## Crew

Commander Charles "Pete" Conrad, command module pilot Richard Gordon and lunar module pilot Alan Bean. Conrad was flying in space for the third time (before that on Gemini 5 and Gemini 11), Gordon for the second (Gemini 11), Bean for the first. The command module was called Yankee Clipper, the lunar module Intrepid.

## Lightning at launch

The Saturn V lifted off on November 14, 1969, at 16:22 UTC, in rain and low clouds. Thirty-six seconds after liftoff lightning struck the spacecraft, and 52 seconds after liftoff a second bolt hit. The electrical power cut out for a moment, telemetry was lost, and the systems switched automatically to backup batteries. The crew restored main power, and 11 minutes 44 seconds after liftoff the spacecraft reached its planned orbit around the Earth. On the way to the Moon the lunar module was checked separately: the lightning had not damaged it.

## Flight timeline

Times are UTC, as in the NASA archive.

| Date and time, UTC | Event |
|---|---|
| November 14, 16:22 | Launch from Complex 39A, lightning strikes at 36 and 52 seconds |
| November 14, 19:15 | The third-stage engine sends the spacecraft toward the Moon |
| November 18, 03:47 | Entry into lunar orbit |
| November 19, 04:16 | Intrepid separates from Yankee Clipper |
| November 19, 06:54:35 | Landing in Oceanus Procellarum (Ocean of Storms), at the edge of Surveyor crater |
| November 19-20 | Two moonwalks |
| November 20, 14:25:47 | Liftoff from the Moon after 31 hours 31 minutes on the surface |
| November 20, 17:58 | Docking with Yankee Clipper |
| November 20, 20:21 | The ascent stage is jettisoned and sent toward the Moon |
| November 21, 20:49 | Engine burn for the return to Earth |
| November 24, 20:58:24 | Splashdown in the Pacific Ocean near American Samoa |

From launch to splashdown, 244 hours 36 minutes 24 seconds passed.

## On the surface

Intrepid landed in Oceanus Procellarum (Ocean of Storms), at 3.01° south latitude and 23.42° west longitude. Conrad and Bean went out onto the surface twice, on November 19 and 20. During the first moonwalk they set up the ALSEP science package, which transmitted data to Earth.

[[figure:alsep]]

During the second moonwalk the astronauts walked to Surveyor 3. This is an uncrewed NASA spacecraft that landed on the Moon on April 19, 1967. The lunar module touched down about 600 feet (about 180 metres) from it. The astronauts removed the television camera and several other parts from Surveyor so that on Earth people could see what two and a half years on the Moon had done to the metal, glass and wiring. The camera is now on display at the National Air and Space Museum in Washington.

[[figure:surveyor-conrad]]

[[figure:surveyor]]

In all, the crew collected 34.35 kg of lunar rocks and soil.

## The first artificial moonquake

After docking, the ascent stage of Intrepid was jettisoned and sent toward the Moon. Its impact was recorded by the seismometer the astronauts had left behind: it was the first time an artificially produced moonquake was recorded.

## What the Moon looked like from Earth that day

We calculated the phase for the moment of landing, November 19, 1969 at 06:54:35 UTC. From Earth the Moon was waxing and 78% lit: 9.36 days had passed since the new moon on November 9. The distance from the centre of the Earth to the centre of the Moon was 379,310 km.

## Return

Apollo 12 splashed down on November 24 in the Pacific Ocean, 6.9 km from the aircraft carrier Hornet, which picked up the crew. The Yankee Clipper command module is now on display at the Virginia Air and Space Center in Hampton.
