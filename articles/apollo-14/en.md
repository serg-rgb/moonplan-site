The third Moon landing came almost ten months after the Apollo 13 accident. The commander was Alan Shepard, the first American in space. This time he spent more than a day on the Moon.

## The crew

Commander Alan Shepard, 47, for whom this was a second flight after Mercury-Redstone 3. Command module pilot Stuart Roosa, 37, and lunar module pilot Edgar Mitchell, 40, were flying for the first time. The command module was called Kitty Hawk and the lunar module Antares. The backup crew: Eugene Cernan, Ronald Evans and Joe Engle.

[[figure:white-room]]

## Launch and six docking attempts

Because of clouds and rain, the launch was delayed by 40 minutes 2 seconds. The Saturn V lifted off on January 31, 1971 at 21:03:02 UTC. To reach the Moon on schedule, the first trajectory correction was made earlier than planned.

[[figure:launch]]

Then the docking problems began. The command module was supposed to turn around and dock with the lunar module. Five attempts failed: the latches of the docking ring did not engage. The sixth attempt, on February 1 at 02:00:02 UTC, succeeded, and the mechanism did not fail again.

From launch to splashdown, 216 hours 1 minute 58 seconds passed.

## On the surface

Antares landed in hilly terrain 24 km north of the rim of Fra Mauro crater, at 3.65° S, 17.47° W. Shepard and Mitchell went out onto the surface twice, on February 5 and 6, for 9 hours 23 minutes in total.

[[figure:flag]]

[[figure:lm]]

They set up the ALSEP science package, collected 42.28 kg of samples and took many photographs. This time they brought a hand cart, a "rickshaw" (MET), for tools and samples, which was used only on this expedition.

[[figure:met]]

At the end of the second moonwalk, Shepard hit two golf balls.

## What the Moon looked like from Earth that day

We calculated the phase for the moment of landing, February 5, 1971 at 09:18:11 UTC. From Earth the Moon was waxing and 78% lit: 9.43 days had passed since the new moon on January 26. The distance from the centre of the Earth to the centre of the Moon was 389,067 km.

## The return

After the samples were transferred, the ascent stage was separated and sent toward the Moon, where it crashed on February 8. Apollo 14 splashed down on February 9 at 21:05 UTC in the Pacific Ocean, about 765 nautical miles south of American Samoa. The crew was picked up by the USS New Orleans. This was the last Apollo expedition after which the astronauts were placed in quarantine. The Kitty Hawk command module is on display at the Astronaut Hall of Fame in Titusville, Florida.
