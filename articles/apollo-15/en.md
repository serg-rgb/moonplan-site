Apollo 15 opened a new series of expeditions designed for long work on the Moon. For the first time, astronauts took a lunar rover with them. In it, David Scott and James Irwin drove 27.9 km at the foot of the Apennine mountains and along Hadley Rille, and spent almost three days on the Moon.

## The crew

Commander David Scott, 39, was flying into space for the third time (before that, Gemini 8 and Apollo 9). Command module pilot Alfred Worden, 39, and lunar module pilot James Irwin, 41, were flying for the first time. The command module was called Endeavor and the lunar module Falcon. The backup crew: Richard Gordon, Vance Brand and Harrison Schmitt.

## Problems on the way

The Saturn V lifted off on July 26, 1971 at 13:34 UTC. On the way to the Moon, a short circuit was found in the main engine system of the service module, and backup procedures were developed for using it. It also turned out that the outer glass of one of the lunar module's sensors had broken, and a small water leak appeared in the command module, which had to be fixed and mopped up.

On July 30 the lunar module could not undock right away. Worden found a loose cable connector and plugged it back in, and at 18:13:30 UTC Falcon separated.

## Landing and the lunar rover

Falcon landed on July 30 at 22:16:29 UTC at the foot of the Apennine mountains, at 26.1° N, 3.6° E.

[[figure:falcon]]

This was the first expedition with a lunar rover. In it the astronauts drove over the terrain within 5 km of the lunar module. In three moonwalks lasting 18 hours 35 minutes in total, they drove 27.9 km, collected 77.31 kg of rock and soil, set up the ALSEP science instruments and took many photographs.

## The hammer and the feather

After the last moonwalk, Scott demonstrated an experiment on live television: he dropped a hammer and a feather at the same time. In the lunar vacuum they fell at the same speed, just as mechanics without air resistance requires.

## The first spacewalk far from Earth

On the way back, on August 5, Alfred Worden opened the hatch of the command module and made three trips to the scientific instrument bay at the rear of the service module to retrieve the film cassettes and inspect the equipment. This was the first spacewalk far from Earth, and it lasted 38 minutes 12 seconds.

Before that, on August 4, a small science satellite was pushed out of the same bay by a spring into lunar orbit.

## What the Moon looked like from Earth that day

We calculated the phase for the moment of landing, July 30, 1971 at 22:16:29 UTC. From Earth the Moon was waxing and 55% lit, slightly more than first quarter: 8.54 days had passed since the new moon on July 22. The distance from the centre of the Earth to the centre of the Moon was 399,983 km.

## The return

During the descent, one of the three main parachutes did not open fully, and the command module came down at 35 km/h, 4.5 km/h faster than planned. Apollo 15 splashed down on August 7 at 20:45:53 UTC in the Pacific Ocean, 330 miles north of Honolulu, 9.8 km from the USS Okinawa. The Endeavor command module is on display at the National Museum of the US Air Force at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base in Dayton, Ohio.
