Apollo 1 never flew. The first crewed spacecraft of the program was due to launch on February 21, 1967, but more than three weeks earlier, during a ground test, a fire broke out in the cabin. Virgil Grissom, Edward White and Roger Chaffee died.

## The crew

Virgil Grissom was named commander of the AS-204 flight, and Edward White and Roger Chaffee were to fly with him. The backups were Walter Schirra, Donn Eisele and Walter Cunningham. In October 1968 they were the ones who flew Apollo 7.

## January 27, 1967

At Launch Complex 34 at Cape Kennedy, a full check of the spacecraft with its rocket was under way. As on launch day, the crew and the ground teams closed all three hatches and filled the cabin with pure oxygen at a pressure of 16.7 pounds per square inch, slightly above atmospheric pressure.

According to the board's timeline, at 23:30:54.8 GMT the instruments recorded a noticeable voltage spike. Ten seconds later, at 23:31:04.7, the crew sent the first report of a fire in the command module. At 23:31:19.4 the cabin shell ruptured under pressure, and three seconds later all voice and data transmission ceased.

[[figure:patch]]

## What the board found

The investigators found traces of arcing in several wires under Grissom's left seat, but could not establish the exact source of ignition. According to NASA, the fire spread very quickly because of the pure oxygen under pressure and because there were more combustible materials in the cabin than was permitted. The hatch also prevented escape: the inner hatch opened inward, and even in ideal conditions it took 90 seconds to open.

In April 1967 the board issued a report of 3,000 pages. The spacecraft was redesigned. At a press conference the astronauts listed the main changes: a new single hatch that opens outward, combustible materials removed or replaced, protected wire bundles and fire-resistant spacesuits. All the following Apollo crews flew a more advanced version of the spacecraft, Block II, rather than the Block I that had been prepared for Apollo 1.

## Why Apollo 1 and not Apollo 204

NASA named the flight that Grissom, White and Chaffee were preparing for Apollo 1. There were never any missions named Apollo 2 and Apollo 3. The next numbers, Apollo 4, 5 and 6, went to uncrewed flights, and the next crewed spacecraft was Apollo 7.

## Launch Complex 34 today

The first Saturn I and Saturn IB rockets lifted off from Complex 34, and Apollo 7 left from here in October 1968. Today only the launch pad remains of the complex.
